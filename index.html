How it's drawn

The pipeline

The whole pavilion is real three.js geometry lit by a sun, a sky bounce and hanging lamps. It is rendered into a small offscreen target — roughly 300 px tall — with anti-aliasing off and nearest sampling. A second full-screen pass then reads that target, its depth buffer and a flag mask.

  • Depth outlines — neighbouring depth samples are compared; silhouettes past a threshold are inked dark. Transparent panes and nets carry a flag, so they are excluded from outline and dither work.
  • OKLab palette — every pixel is converted from linear sRGB into OKLab and snapped to the nearest of 54 fixed entries. Matching in perceptual space keeps the brick, oak and jersey tones separated.
  • Bayer dither — an optional 4×4 threshold nudges the OKLab coordinates before matching, trading banding for texture.
  • Integer upscale — each low-res texel is blown up by an integer pixel-size factor; the page never samples between texels.
  • Pixel-snapped camera — the orthographic camera orbits in 90° steps and its world offset is quantised to the low-res grid so walls do not shimmer.
  • Raw 3D — bypasses the palette, outline and dither passes to show the geometry as it really is.

The palette

54 OKLab entries.

The simulation

Rules, AI, ball physics and the cup schedule run on a fixed 60 Hz step and draw from a seeded generator, so the same seed plus the same input tends to produce the same cup. Release noise grows with distance and contest: makes and misses fall out of the physics rather than a dice roll on the scoreboard.

Parquet squeaks are painted into a live canvas under pivots and stops; the polisher erases that canvas between games and leaves a faint shine trail of its own.
